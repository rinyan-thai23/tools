You are writing an article or a book and want a number like "how Japan's population moved over ten years", but you do not know where a citable public dataset lives, and searching stalls. Enter a topic and this tool lists what Google Dataset Search returns: the dataset name, provider, last-updated date and link.

Why use this tool

  • Take the search results away as a table. Name, provider, update date and link sit side by side, a good first pass before you compare candidates.
  • About $0.002 for 20 results. About 500 searches per $1, so you can try many topics.
  • About 3 seconds. That is what we measured.
  • The update date is visible. It helps you avoid stale data (some rows are blank).
  • Save as CSV. Keep a list of source links for later.

When it is not the right fit

  • You want to see the data itself → the table shows only name, provider, update date and link. Check the numbers on the provider's page.
  • You want only Japanese official statistics → e-Stat is more focused. For our sample "population japan", private aggregation sites led the list.
  • You need the usage terms → the table does not show them. Read the terms on the linked page before you use anything.
  • Results for Japanese keywords → we tested English only.

This tool does one thing: list where public datasets on a topic are published.

FAQ

May I put the data in my article or book as is?

Terms differ per dataset. This tool does not judge it. Check the usage terms and the citation style at the provider.

Why are some update dates or providers blank?

We show exactly what the API returned. In our sample, 4 of 20 rows had no provider and 1 had no update date.

Why is there no country or language selector?

The API works from the keyword alone.

Can I choose the number of results?

It is fixed at 20. At 40 the cost was about $0.004 (measured).

Can I sign up with a free email address?

No. DataForSEO does not accept free email providers. See What is DataForSEO?
