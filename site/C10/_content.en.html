Why use this tool

  • Look up by shop name. Even a short brand name like "nike" returned 7 advertiser rows in our sample, including look-alike names such as Nike Klara and Nikel Kola.
  • An ad-count estimate. A rough sense of how seriously an advertiser spends.
  • About $0.002 and a few seconds. Our "nike" lookup took about 0.6 seconds. About 500 lookups per $1, so you can check several rivals in a row.
  • Pick a country. Choose the US or another market (you cannot narrow to a city or area).
  • Save as CSV. Advertiser IDs come along.

When it is not the right fit

  • You want to search by domain → this tool looks up a name. Google also returns domain-name matches (10 of the 17 rows in our "nike" sample), but they carry no advertiser ID or ad count, so the table leaves them out.
  • You want to see the ads → the table has advertiser info only. Use the "see running ads" tool or Google's Transparency Center.
  • You need an exact ad count → the number is the API's approximate value.
  • A large company may show many advertiser IDs in one row (in our sample, "Nike, Inc." was one row listing two advertiser IDs, about 10,003 ads in total).

This tool does one thing: list the advertisers registered with Google under a name.

FAQ

What if the shop does not run ads?

You will see "No results found". That does not prove they run no ads; try another spelling.

Is the ad count the number of ads running now?

It is the API's approximate figure, shown as is. This tool cannot say whether it is current.

What is the advertiser ID for?

It is a key for finding that advertiser's ads in Google's Transparency Center.

Why does one company show several advertiser IDs?

Large companies can hold several advertiser accounts. In our "nike" lookup, the "Nike, Inc." row listed two IDs.
